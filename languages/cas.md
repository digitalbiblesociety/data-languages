Chimané (Tsimaneʼ) and Mosetén are dialects of a South American language isolate. Mosetén is further divided into Mosetén of Santa Ana and Mosetén of Covendo. Chimane is a language of the western Bolivian lowlands spoken by the Tsimane peoples along the Beni River and the region around San Borja in the Department of Beni (Bolivia). Sakel (2004) classifies them as two languages for a number of reasons, yet some of the variants of the language are mutually intelligible and they reportedly have no trouble communicating and were evidently a single language separated recently through cultural contact.
