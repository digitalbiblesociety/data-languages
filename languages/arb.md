Modern Standard Arabic is the formal written variety of Arabic, derived from Classical Arabic and used across the Arab world for literature, broadcasting, and education. The Quran, the central religious text of Islam, was written in Classical Arabic in the seventh century CE and gave the language its enduring religious and literary status. The spoken vernaculars of the Arab world, from Maghrebi to Levantine to Gulf and Egyptian Arabic, diverge substantially from Modern Standard Arabic and from each other.
