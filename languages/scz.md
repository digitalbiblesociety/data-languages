Shetland dialect is a mixed language spoken in Shetland, an archipelago to the north of mainland Scotland. The exact number of speakers is not known, since it has to date never been included in any census. It emerged through the long-drawn and stable bilingualism of Norn and Lowlands Scots brought to Shetland from the early 15th century and onwards. Norn is an extinct North Germanic language, descended from Western Norse, which was spoken in Shetland until the late 19th century, though as of 2025, living memory reports the last known speaker to have died as late as 1925. This long-drawn contact situation resulted in a very distinct linguistic blend of Norn and Lowland Scots, with a noticeable contact influence of Low Germanic languages. It has been recognised as a highly distinct variety for centuries. The language retains many words of Norn origin. Many of them, if they are not place-names, refer to e.g. seasons, weather, topographical features, sea scapes, fishing, parts of boats and boat building, agriculture and crofting, crops and livestock, wool handling and knitting, peat cutting, stone building, music instrument building, and so on.
